# Zen Garden

Status: brief (2026-09-24). A chill toy (docs/12 shelf F): a tabletop karesansui, the dry rock garden of Japanese temples (Ryōan-ji in Kyoto is the famous one) shrunk to a tray: rake the sand into patterns and set a few stones. Nobody's product.

## What makes it feel right
1. The rake leaving four clean grooves behind your finger.
2. Raking a ring round a stone.
3. Smoothing it all flat and starting again.

## Our design
- **A tray of sand**, drawn warm and flat. **Drag to rake**: four tines follow your finger, each groove a darker line with a lit edge beside it, so it reads as cut into the sand. A soft scrape now and then.
- **Stone**: tap the sand to set a stone or lift it; up to seven, each its own shape and grey, with the sand already raked in a ring round it.
- **Smooth** rakes it flat again (the grooves fade). **Done** when you like it; nothing to win or lose.
- **Keyboard**: arrows move, Enter sets a stone, R rakes a line across the row, S smooths, D is done.
- **The rules keep only the stones** and the moment you say it is done. The raking is drawn by the scene, like a drawing in Draw & Guess, so a replay shows the stones but not the lines.

## Tests
A stone goes down and comes up; each new stone is the next shape, up to seven, and lifting one makes room; done finishes it; test play finishes. Invariant, every move: never more than seven stones, and a move only touches one cell.
